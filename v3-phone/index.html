Input device

Calibration detects which stick axis is throttle, yaw, pitch and roll, their full range and direction, then saves it in this browser. Throttle is read as absolute — it never springs back.

Xbox / PlayStation controller

Controller type
Controller layout Throttle

Game-controller sticks spring back to the middle, so by default the middle of the throttle stick is hover: push up to climb, pull down to drop. Auto-detect treats Xbox, PlayStation and other standard controllers as game controllers and keeps FPV radios (and anything you've calibrated) on absolute throttle.

Switches

Live stick check

Check: right stick up = throttle up (and it stays), right stick right = yaw right, left stick forward = nose down, left stick right = roll right (Mode 3).

Phone controller

A friend can fly with their phone — no app, no cable, no Bluetooth, and it doesn't need to be on the same Wi-Fi. They open a web page and type a 4-letter code (or scan a QR code). Two layouts on the phone: two thumbsticks, or Mickey Mode — hold the phone upright and point it like a DJI motion controller: the camera looks where you point and the throttle flies you that way, with a thumb pad to strafe and trim height. Best in Easy Mode or Easy Acro.

Flight mode

Manual is real acro: the sticks set how fast the drone rotates (the rates below), and it holds whatever angle you leave it at — flips, rolls, everything. Easy Mode works like Betaflight's ANGLE mode: the pitch and roll sticks tilt the drone up to 35°, and it levels itself when you let go. Yaw and throttle work the same in both. No flips or rolls in Easy Mode. Easy Acro is Easy Mode plus one-button tricks — the drone flies the whole trick itself and levels out after: 1 front flip · 2 back flip · 3 roll left · 4 roll right · 5 power loop · 6 juicy flick · 7 180 (also the game controller's D-pad for flips and rolls, and the phone's trick pad). It's a setting for you only — in online rooms nobody else's mode changes. Switch any time with M or the mode button on the flight screen.
